# MODULE 1: FOUNDATION

## Overview

This module establishes the business infrastructure you need before offering AI-assisted contract-writing services. Across three procedures, you will set up your business accounts, secure a domain and professional email, and prepare the foundational tools for a workflow built around ChatGPT, Make.com, and Loom. You will also define a basic separation between your business operations and personal accounts, so client information, access credentials, and operational records have a clear home from the start.

Foundation work matters because contract-writing services depend on trust, consistent communication, and careful handling of sensitive information. A polished domain and business email help clients recognize a legitimate service; organized tool accounts make it easier to build and maintain automations; and clear operating boundaries reduce the chance of exposing client data or losing access to essential systems. AI-generated contract language is not a substitute for qualified legal advice, so your setup should support transparent service terms, human review, and appropriate client expectations.

Skipping this module can leave you with scattered logins, unreliable communication, avoidable security gaps, and workflow rework once clients arrive. Complete the setup before building automations or accepting paid work. Tool prices and free-tier limits can change; check each provider’s current plan details before subscribing.

| Tool | Purpose | Free Tier | Paid Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Drafting and refining contract-related content for human review | Free plan available; usage and features are limited | ChatGPT Plus is typically $20/month; verify current pricing |
| Make.com | Connecting tools and automating repeatable business workflows | Free plan available with limited monthly credits and features | Paid plans vary by operations and features; verify current pricing |
| Loom | Recording client-facing explanations, walkthroughs, and internal process videos | Free Starter plan available with recording and storage limits | Paid plans vary by features and billing term; verify current pricing |
| Domain registrar and email provider | Establishing a branded web address and professional email | Some providers offer limited email trials or bundled entry-level options; a domain is generally paid | Domain renewals and business email subscriptions vary by provider and plan |

**Estimated time to complete:** 2–4 hours, excluding any delays for domain verification, account approval, or email DNS propagation.

## Procedure 1.1: Register Your Business Domain on Hostinger

1. Open [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/) in your browser. Select **Sign up** or **Log in** in the upper-right corner. Create your account with an email address you control, or sign in to your existing Hostinger account.

2. Open a new tab and choose a short, professional business name for your AI contract-writing service. Check that the name is easy to spell and does not imply that you are a law firm or provide legal advice unless you are authorized to do so.

3. Return to Hostinger and select **Domains** from the main navigation, then choose **Domain Name Search**. If you do not see that path, use Hostinger’s domain search at [hostinger.com/domain-name-search](https://www.hostinger.com/domain-name-search).

4. Enter your preferred domain in the search box without `https://` or `www`—for example, `clearcontractstudio.com`—and select **Search**. Review the available extensions and first-year price shown on the results page. Hostinger pricing changes by extension, promotion, and location; rely on the price displayed at checkout rather than an advertised starting price.

5. Compare the domain options before buying. A `.com` is commonly recognized, while other extensions may cost more or be less familiar to clients. Do not buy multiple extensions unless you have a specific reason to protect your brand.

| Option | Typical use | Cost check |
|---|---|---|
| `.com` | Broad, general business use | Confirm Hostinger’s current checkout price |
| `.co` | Alternative when the `.com` is unavailable | Check renewal price; it may differ from the first-year price |
| `.ai` | AI-focused branding | Check both registration and renewal price; it can cost substantially more |
| Hostinger email | Business email on your domain | Availability, included period, and ongoing price depend on the hosting plan and current offer |

6. Select **Add to cart** beside the domain you want. Before proceeding, check the spelling character by character. Avoid hyphens, confusing abbreviations, and names that are difficult to say aloud.

7. Select **Continue** or **Checkout**. On the order summary, check the registration period, first-term price, renewal price, and any add-ons. Remove website hosting or other extras if your immediate goal is only to register the domain.

8. Review optional items such as **WHOIS privacy** or domain protection. If Hostinger offers privacy protection for this domain, read the terms and price before enabling it. Do not assume a feature is free or included; the checkout summary is the source of truth.

9. Sign in to your Hostinger account when prompted. If you are a new customer, enter your name, email address, and password in the displayed fields, then select **Create account**. Use a unique password and store it in a password manager.

10. Enter your billing details exactly as required by the payment form. Select a payment method, enter the payment information, and review the order total, taxes, renewal terms, and any recurring charges.

**Check-in:** Do you see the correct domain, registration period, and total price in the order summary? If not, select **Back to cart**, remove unwanted add-ons, and recheck the domain spelling and renewal price before paying.

11. Select **Submit secure payment**, **Pay now**, or the payment button shown at checkout. The exact label can vary by country and payment method. Wait for Hostinger to confirm payment; do not refresh the page while the transaction is processing.

12. Confirm that the order-success screen lists your domain. You should also receive a payment or order confirmation at the email address used for your Hostinger account. Save the receipt and note the domain’s renewal date.

13. Open [hpanel.hostinger.com](https://hpanel.hostinger.com/) and sign in. From the dashboard, open **Domains** and locate the new domain. Select **Manage** beside it.

14. Complete domain ownership verification if Hostinger prompts you to do so. Open the verification email sent to the registrant email address, then select the verification link. If you do not see it, check spam and promotions folders, and use the resend option in Hostinger’s domain-management screen.

**Check-in:** Do you see your domain listed in Hostinger’s **Domains** area with a status such as active or registered? If not, check **Billing → Orders** for the transaction status. If the order is pending, follow Hostinger’s payment instructions; if it is missing, contact Hostinger support with your order number.

15. Review the domain’s renewal settings in its management panel. Confirm the renewal date and whether auto-renew is enabled. Keep auto-renew on only if the payment method will remain valid and you intend to keep the domain; set a calendar reminder as a backup.

16. Enable account security. In Hostinger, open your profile or account settings, choose **Security**, and enable two-factor authentication if available. Store recovery codes securely. Do not share your Hostinger login with contractors or automation tools.

17. Do not connect Make.com, Loom, ChatGPT, or a contract-generation workflow to the domain until you have decided what the domain will host. Registration reserves the name; it does not automatically publish a website, create business email, or make the domain legally protect your business name.

18. If you see **“Domain unavailable”**, the name was already registered or is restricted. Try a distinct variation—such as adding “studio” or “services”—and search again. If you see **“Payment failed”**, the payment method may have declined the transaction; verify the billing address, try another payment method, and check whether a pending charge already exists before submitting again. If you see **“Verification required”**, follow the verification email link; the domain may not function normally until verification is complete.

19. Record these details in a secure business operations document: domain name, Hostinger account email, order number, registration date, renewal date, and whether auto-renew is enabled. Do not record your password or payment-card details in that document.

20. Finish by testing the domain in your browser using `https://yourdomain.com` and `http://yourdomain.com`. It may show an error or parking page because no website or DNS destination has been configured yet. That is expected after registration; the milestone is complete when the domain appears in Hostinger and ownership verification is finished.

# MODULE 2: TECH STACK

## Overview

This module turns your AI contract-writing idea into a connected, testable system. You will select the tools that handle intake, drafting, review, automation, and client handoff; connect them; configure required API keys and permissions; and verify that information moves correctly between each part of the stack. The goal is not merely to collect software. It is to establish a reliable workflow in which a client’s approved inputs can trigger a draft, route it for human review, and produce a clear handoff without silently dropping data.

This matters because contract work depends on accurate inputs, controlled access, and reviewable outputs. A broken connection can leave a client waiting, send incomplete information into a draft, or expose confidential material to the wrong destination. Skipping this module means building later automation on untested assumptions. Do not treat AI-generated contract language as legal advice or a substitute for qualified legal review; use your system to organize drafting and review, and set clear boundaries with clients.

| Tool | Purpose | Free Tier | Paid Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Generate and refine draft language from approved inputs | Free plan available; features and limits can change | ChatGPT Plus is listed at $20/month in the United States; API usage is billed separately |
| Make.com | Connect intake, AI drafting, notifications, and file workflows | Free plan includes 1,000 credits/month, subject to current plan terms | Paid plans start at $9/month for Core with annual billing; pricing can change |

**Check-in:** Do you see an `OpenAI` row with `Status: Active`, a rotation date, and no secret value? If not, correct the row before moving on. If the page is publicly accessible, turn off **Share to web** and remove public access immediately.

## C.1 Set the Planning Assumptions

The projections below model a solo operator selling AI-assisted contract drafting and workflow automation. “Clients” means paying client accounts active during that month—not cumulative sign-ups. A client may buy a one-time package, a monthly subscription, or both.

The model assumes:

- Prices are in USD and are collected before delivery begins.
- Revenue is recognized in the month the work is delivered.
- Monthly expenses include software, payment fees, lead generation, contractor support, and operating costs.
- Profit is revenue minus the listed expenses. It is **before income tax, owner compensation, and any unlisted costs**.
- Initial investment is a separate one-time amount of **$1,500** for setup, domain, initial software, templates, and launch assets.
- Refunds, unpaid invoices, and churn are not included. Add them to your own model if they apply.

Use Make.com to send paid invoice and delivery records into a Notion revenue database. Use Loom to record walkthroughs and client handoffs; include the time and contractor cost of producing those walkthroughs in your expense assumptions. Track your actual figures monthly rather than relying on projections.

## C.3 Pricing Tiers

**Margin formula:**  
`(Price − direct delivery cost) ÷ Price × 100`

Direct delivery cost includes contractor hours, document review, payment-processing fees, AI usage, and other costs directly tied to fulfillment. For example, if the Business Pack costs $280 to deliver, its margin is `($799 − $280) ÷ $799 = 65%` after rounding.
