## The Workflow: Step-by-Step With Every Shortcut

### Step 1: Prospect Data Harvest (1 h)

Start by firing up Apollo.io. Pick a niche—tech startups, SaaS, or local brick‑and‑mortar. In Apollo, set the filter: “Company size 10‑50, revenue $5M‑$50M, location US.” Export the list as a CSV. Save it to [Notion](https://notion.so/) for quick reference—one column per field, another for notes.  

Now plug the CSV into PhantomBuster. Use the “LinkedIn Lead Gen” script to pull LinkedIn URLs, emails, and titles. Hit the 5‑minute run, then download the JSON. You’ll get 200 leads in 8 minutes. That’s a 3‑fold boost over manual hunting.  

**HACK**: Map the JSON to a Google Sheet via [Make.com](https://www.make.com/en/register?pc=menshly). Set a Make.com workflow that pulls new rows every hour, updates the sheet, and flags duplicates. This keeps your list fresh without manual sifting.  

### Step 2: Lead Qualification & Enrichment (45 min)

Open the sheet and let Apollo’s “Enrichment” feature auto‑populate missing emails and phone numbers. For the 20% that still lack data, fire up [Replit](https://replit.com/refer/egwuokwor) and run a small script that scrapes company websites for contact forms. Save the output back to the sheet.  

Use [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/) to run a quick “Domain Overview” on each company to gauge traffic and keywords. If the traffic is below 5K/month, tag the lead as low‑priority. Store all tags in Notion.  

**HACK**: Write a prompt for ChatGPT: “Create a 2‑sentence pitch for a SaaS company with <5k traffic that focuses on lead capture.” Feed the pitch into the sheet as a ready‑to‑send line. This saves you 10 minutes per email.  

### Step 3: Outreach Campaign Build (1 h)

Create an email sequence in ActiveCampaign. Use Apollo’s SMTP to bypass spam. Draft the first email with ChatGPT: “Subject: Quick win for [Company]—no spam, high ROI.” The body: “Hey [FirstName], I noticed your site’s traffic dipped in April; here’s a quick audit that could help.” Paste the template, then use Zapier to pull the recipients from the sheet into the sequence.  

Add a Loom video for the second touch. Record a 30‑second demo of your lead‑gen framework, then upload it to [Fliki AI](https://fliki.ai?referral=noah-wilson-w84be4) to add brand colors and captions. Attach the Loom link in the email.  

**HACK**: Use [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) to design a single branded logo that appears in every email signature. Save the PNG to your Dropbox, then use Zapier to auto‑embed it in ActiveCampaign.  

### Step 4: Follow‑Up & Client Handoff (30 min)

Set up a Zap in Zapier: when a lead opens an email, trigger a Slack notification to your “Follow‑Up” channel. Use Apollo’s tagging to move the lead to “Hot” once they reply.  

Schedule a Calendly link in the third email for the client to book a 15‑minute call. Add a reminder that [Vapi](https://vapi.ai/) will send a voice message if they miss the slot. Vapi can auto‑record the same script you used for the email.  

After the call, drop the client into a new Notion board called “Clients.” Attach the signed engagement PDF, set a due date for the first deliverable, and ping the team via Slack.  

**HACK**: Use the “ActiveCampaign → Notion” integration to automatically create a new page for each client, pulling in contact details and initial objectives. This eliminates the need to copy‑paste manually.

## What Nobody Warns You About

**1. Apollo’s Contact Limits Force a “Hard Stop”**  
Apollo’s free tier caps at 1,000 contacts per month. You’ll hit that wall in just 3–4 weeks if you’re pulling leads for 10 clients. The paid plans start at $99/mo for 10,000 contacts, then $199/mo for 100,000. Even then, you’re burning $1.00 per contact every month. If you want 200,000 leads a month, you’re staring at $400/month just for the database, not the time you’ll spend cleaning it. That money eats into your $28k/month target faster than you think. So, budget for database expansion from day one.

**2. PhantomBuster Traps Lead to a “Speed‑to‑Failure” Loop**  
PhantomBuster is great for scraping LinkedIn, but their usage limits are strict—roughly 5,000 LinkedIn actions per month for the free tier. Paid plans lift that to 15,000 for $199/mo, but each action costs a credit. If you rely on 30,000 actions to feed Apollo, you’ll be paying $200/month just for scraping. That’s a hidden overhead you’ll only notice when your profit margin shrinks. You can’t afford to hit the limit and then scramble to get data from scratch.

**3. Email Outreach Is a Cost‑Per‑Click Minefield**  
Klaviyo and ActiveCampaign both charge per contact stored. Klaviyo starts at $30/month for 500 contacts, jumps to $150/month for 2,500, and then $400/month for 10,000. ActiveCampaign is similar: $29/month for 500, $69/month for 1,500, $149/month for 3,000. Keeping 10,000 contacts in an email list for a single client will balloon your overhead to $500–$600/month. Add the cost of sending 1,000 emails a month (most plans bill by volume) and you’re looking at $1–$1.5k in email costs alone—diminishing your profit.

**4. Content Creation Is a Time Sink, Not a Money Sink**  
You may think tools like Canva, Fliki AI, or Midjourney can replace human creativity, but they’re not instant. Canva’s Pro plan is $12/month, Fliki AI charges $30/month for 5000 words of video, and Midjourney’s subscription is $10/month per user. Even with these, you still need to script, design, and edit—usually 2–3 hours per email or video. If you outsource to a freelancer or a 3rd‑party agency, expect $50–$80/hour. That’s a big chunk of your $28k/month that disappears if you don’t automate or delegate efficiently.
