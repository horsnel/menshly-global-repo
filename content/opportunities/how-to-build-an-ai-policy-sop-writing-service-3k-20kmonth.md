**The Free Stack: Starting With Zero Dollars**

You can spin up an SOP‑writing service on a shoestring budget. Below are seven tools that cost nothing to get you off the ground—just keep in mind the speed bumps that come with free tiers.

- [**Canva — $0**](https://www.canva.com/) – Create professional‑looking policy covers and infographics without hiring a designer.  
- **ChatGPT — $0** – Use the free page‑limit to generate first‑draft policy language; just remember the 3‑month free trial on the paid plan.  
- **Loom — $0** – Record quick video walkthroughs of SOPs for clients; the free plan gives you 25 min per video.  
- **Zapier — $0** – Automate data flow from email to Notion and Google Sheets, but the free plan caps you at 100 tasks/month.  
- **Hostinger — $0** – Launch a basic website with 1 GB storage and a free sub‑domain; perfect for a portfolio site until you grow.  
- [**Make.com — $0**](https://www.make.com/en/register?pc=menshly) – Orchestrate multi‑app workflows (e.g., pull a client brief from a Google Sheet, feed it into ChatGPT, dump the result into Notion).  

[accent-box]**Hack:** Create a SOP skeleton in Notion, then hit the “+” button, choose “ChatGPT”, and instantly fill in the blanks with AI‑crafted prose. It saves a dozen hours per SOP. [/accent-box]

**Limitations & When to Upgrade**

Every free tier is a trade‑off. Notion’s free plan limits you to 1 MiB of attachments; Canva only lets you export to JPEG/PNG at 1080p; ChatGPT’s free tier caps you at 3 M tokens per month, which can stall if you’re drafting policy sets for dozens of clients. Loom’s 25‑minute cap forces you to chop longer walkthroughs into bite‑sized bits. Zapier’s 100‑task/month ceiling means you’ll hit a wall once you automate intake, billing, and SOP delivery. Hostinger’s free sub‑domain looks unprofessional to serious clients, and the bandwidth may drop when traffic spikes. Make.com’s free plan limits you to 10,000 operations per month and blocks premium apps.

When your client list grows past 5–10 monthly retainer accounts, or if you need to deliver custom video walkthroughs longer than 25 min, it’s time to move to the paid tiers. Upgrading unlocks higher task limits, priority support, and the ability to brand your own sub‑domain—things that make clients feel you’re a full‑fledged professional, not a hobbyist.

## What Nobody Warns You About

**1. The Real Cost of “Polished” PDFs**  
You’ll think a free PDF generator will do. It won’t. Clients will see a PDF that looks like a Fortune 500 manual. Canva Pro is $12/month or $120/year if you want brand kits, custom fonts, and unlimited uploads. Add a PDF‑to‑audio layer with ElevenLabs – $15/month for 30,000 chars. If you outsource a designer, it’s $500+ per doc. Expect to spend 15‑20% of the bill on production tools alone.

**2. The Compliance “Black Hole”**  
Every policy you write is a legal document. A quick Google search for “data‑processing SOP” shows you need to reference GDPR, CCPA, or state laws. You can scrape the legislation with a script on Replit for $0, but you still need a lawyer to audit it. A small law firm will charge $200–$400 per hour. If you skip that step, you’ll lose a client for a single clause. Plan on allocating 30% of the project cost for a compliance check.

**3. The Automation Overload Trap**  
You want everything to run on autopilot. Zapier’s Starter plan is $19/month, but you’ll likely need $49/month for multi‑step Zaps. Make.com’s Professional tier is $39/month, but you’ll use both. PhantomBuster for LinkedIn outreach is $79/month, Buffer for social scheduling is $12/month, and you still need Calendly for meetings at $8/month. Add up the hidden monthly cost, and you’re looking at $140/month just to keep the engine running. The more tools, the more integration headaches.

**4. The Revision‑Hell Reality**  
Clients love to flip‑flop. A single policy edit can cost you 30–60 minutes of your time. Track revisions in Notion – that’s free, but the time is real. If you charge $3,000 a month and end up with 4 rounds of edits, you lose 40% of the bill. Use Loom for quick video feedback to cut back-and-forth. Set a hard limit: three revisions, then a “revision fee” of $200 per extra round. Without that, your margins evaporate.
