## The Workflow: Step-by-Step With Every Shortcut

### Step 1: Define Your Offer (15 min)

Open Notion, create a new page called “Service Catalog.” List tiers: Starter ($500), Pro ($1,200), Enterprise ($2,500). Add columns for Delivery Time, Image Count, and Add‑ons (logo, branding guide). Write a super‑short description for each tier in one sentence—no fluff.  
Use the built‑in template gallery to copy a “Pricing” deck and paste it into your page. Update the prices to match your break‑even: Midjourney commercial tier $30/month, Canva Pro $12/month, Make.com Starter $35/month.  
Now you have a living pricing sheet. When a lead calls, just hit “Clone” and you’re ready to quote.  
> **HACK**: Pin the page to your sidebar for instant access. In the “Share” menu, enable “Copy link” and paste it into a Zapier trigger—every new lead gets the exact pricing sheet in their inbox.

### Step 2: Build the Image Production Pipeline (30 min)

Create a new project in Make.com.  
1. **Trigger**: Webhook that fires when a new Notion row is created (use the Notion integration).  
2. **Action**: Send the row data to Midjourney via its API.  
   * Prompt example:  
     `A cyberpunk marketplace at dusk, hyper‑realistic, neon reflection, 4k, 16:9, --ar 16:9 --v 5`  
   * Set “Chaos” to 0.2, “Quality” to 2, “Stylize” to 750.  
   * Use the “Batch” feature to generate 5 variations per prompt.  
3. **Action**: Once Midjourney returns URLs, push them to Canva.  
   * Use Canva’s “Create a design” API to open a new 16:9 canvas, import the Midjourney image, add a brand‑color overlay, and export as PNG.  
4. **Action**: Store the final PNGs in a shared Google Drive folder (or use Hostinger’s SFTP if you’re hosting your own).  
5. **Action**: Send a Loom video link explaining the steps to the client.  
   * Use Loom’s “Create Video” API and set the script: “Your images are ready; click the link below to download.”  

Once set, this chain takes 45 seconds per batch. No more manual copy‑paste.  
> **HACK**: Use Make.com’s “Time delay” module to schedule a second run for re‑renders if the client requests changes—no extra cost or manual click.

### Step 3: Automate Client Delivery & Upsell (20 min)

Hook Zapier into the folder you just created.  
* **Trigger**: New file in Drive.  
* **Action**: Add a new subscriber to Klaviyo with the client’s email.  
* **Action**: Send a custom email (use Klaviyo’s template) with a private download link and a short “Thank you” video from Loom.  
* **Action**: Append a link to your Shopify store that offers a “Packaged Re‑edit” for $199 or a “Brand Asset Bundle” for $499.  

The email should include a CTA: “Want your images in a branded PDF? Click here.”  
Use ActiveCampaign to track opens and clicks; set up a follow‑up drip for clients who don’t purchase the upsell.  

Now you’re not just delivering images—you’re selling higher‑margin add‑ons automatically.

Pull your favorite Midjourney prompts into a Notion database. Use ChatGPT to auto‑generate 5‑word tweaks (e.g., “vintage sunset” → “vintage sunset over a neon city”). No more staring at the same prompt all day.

Subscribe to Canva Pro ($12/mo) and enable the API. Create a brand‑kit template for social posts and marketing collateral. ## What Nobody Warns You About

**Clients Will Keep Asking for Unlimited Edits**  
Every client thinks they get free edits. In reality, each prompt run on Midjourney costs you $1.50 (Pro plan) plus 10 minutes of your time. If you do 30 jobs a month and they each get 3 edits, you’re looking at 90 runs—$135 in software alone. Add 15 minutes per edit, and you’re working 22.5 hours that month. Put a cap at three revisions and charge $50 per extra. Stop the drill.

**Pricing Is Not a One‑Size‑Fit‑All**  
A $100 image feels sweet, but you’re paying $1.50 for the Midjourney run, $3.99/month for Hostinger, $99/mo for Semrush, $79/mo for Apollo.io, plus $4.99/mo for Canva Pro. That’s $188 in recurring costs just to serve one image. Realistically you need $250–$300 per job to break even, not $100. Charge what covers your stack, otherwise you’ll be swimming in debt.

**Your Portfolio Doesn’t Auto‑Scale**  
Midjourney can churn out 10,000 images a day, but clients want a signature look. Each tweak takes 20–30 minutes. You can’t just drop a batch into Canva and run Buffer. You need to record variations in Loom—$80/mo for Loom Pro—and upload them to a Notion gallery for easy client review. That’s an extra 5 hours a week just for polishing.

**Legal & Copyright Slip‑Ups Are Costly**  
Most people ignore that AI prompts can infringe. Midjourney’s commercial license is $79/mo, but copyright claims can still land you in court. Canva stock images have per‑image fees, and using a brand name in a prompt can trigger a takedown. Use a contract template in Notion and track IP. A single lawsuit could cost $10k+ in legal fees, wiping out months of profit.
