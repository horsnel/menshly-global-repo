## The Free Stack: Starting With Zero Dollars

[**Notion — $0**](https://notion.so/) — All‑in‑one workspace for notes, tasks, and docs.  
[**Canva — $0**](https://www.canva.com/) — Design platform with free templates, images, and brand kit tools.  
**Loom — $0** — Record quick video messages up to 5 minutes; great for demo clips or outreach.  
**Calendly — $0** — Schedule calls with a single event type; integrates with Google Calendar.  
**Zapier — $0** — Connect apps and automate 100 tasks/month on the free tier.  
**ChatGPT — $0** — GPT‑4 model for content brainstorming and drafting; limited to 3 hours/month.  
[**Grammarly — $0**](https://grammarly.com/) — Basic writing assistant for grammar and clarity; advanced style checks are paid.

The free stack gets you a skeleton, but it’s not a full‑blown launchpad. Notion’s 5 GB limit means you’ll hit space constraints fast if you’re storing PDFs and long‑form drafts. Canva’s free tier locks premium images behind a paywall; you’ll need to pay after a while to keep your visuals fresh. Loom’s 5‑minute cap forces you to split longer videos into chunks, which can break flow. Calendly’s single‑event limitation makes it hard to offer multiple consultation slots without upgrading. Zapier’s 100‑task/month ceiling is fine for a handful of automations, but any bulk email send or lead‑generation process will exhaust it quickly. ChatGPT’s 3‑hour window forces you to ration your content creation, and you’ll hit the ceiling if you’re drafting multiple newsletters a week. Grammarly’s free mode only flags basic errors; the tone‑style engine that catches awkward phrasing is behind the pro paywall.

When to upgrade? As soon as you hit any of the free tier ceilings, you’re bottlenecking growth. For example, once your subscriber list climbs to 5,000, Beehiiv’s free plan stops delivering to new users. At that point, moving to Beehiiv’s paid tier (starting at $69/month) unlocks higher send limits, advanced templates, and A/B testing. If you’re automating lead flows, Zapier’s 2,000‑task plan ($19.99/month) gives you the flexibility to push data into Klaviyo or ActiveCampaign without manual intervention. When you need high‑volume AI output, consider ChatGPT’s paid plan ($20/1 k tokens) or rehosting on Replit to run your own prompt‑engineering scripts. In short, the free stack is a launchpad, but the real revenue comes once you pay for the scale you need.

## The Workflow: Step-by-Step With Every Shortcut

### Step 1: Nail Your Audience Map (2‑3 hrs)

Start with a quick audit of the niche you’re gunning for. Use **Semrush** to pull top keywords, search volume, and CPC. Hit the “Keyword Magic Tool,” search “AI content marketing,” and export the top 50 terms. Open the list in **Notion**, split it into “High Intent,” “Low Competition,” and “Audience Pain.” This gives you a map of what people actually want.

Next, run a LinkedIn scrape with **PhantomBuster**. Use the “LinkedIn Profile Scraper” to pull 200+ profiles who posted about AI newsletters in the last 30 days. Export to CSV and load into **Apollo.io**. Filter for “Job Title: Content Marketer,” “Company Size: 1‑50,” “Location: US.” You’ll now have a ready‑to‑contact list of prospects who are already talking about newsletters.

HACK: If the CSV is too big for Apollo, chunk it at 100 rows each. Apollo will auto‑dedupe and flag leads with a higher engagement score, saving you time on cold outreach.

### Step 2: Build the AI Content Engine (4‑5 hrs)

Pull **ChatGPT** into a **Replit** project. Spin up a fresh Python app, add the OpenAI key, and write a script that takes a prompt and spits out a 1‑page newsletter. Use the following prompt template:

```
You are an AI newsletter writer. Draft a 700‑word newsletter on “AI in e‑commerce for 2026.” Include a hook, data point, actionable tip, and a CTA to a paid plan. Keep tone conversational. End with a short teaser for next issue.
```

Set **ChatGPT**’s temperature to 0.7 for creativity, max tokens 1000. Wrap the API call in a function that accepts a keyword, runs the prompt, and writes the output to a Markdown file.

Now, automate the flow with **Make.com**. Create a scenario that watches a Google Sheet for new keywords, triggers the Replit script, and pushes the resulting Markdown to **Canva** via API. In Canva, use the “Newsletter” template, import the Markdown, and let the AI‑generated text fill the layout. Adjust font size, add a brand logo, and export as a PDF.

HACK: Use Canva’s built‑in “Elements” library to pull in an AI‑generated image from **Midjourney**. In your Replit script, append the Midjourney prompt “/imagine prompt: futuristic AI e‑commerce dashboard, vibrant colors” to the newsletter and fetch the image URL. Canva’s “Image from URL” block will auto‑inject it.

### Step 3: Polish & Brand (2‑3 hrs)

Open the PDF in **Beehiiv** and use its native editor to tweak headers, add your brand colors, and embed a subscription CTA. Beehiiv’s pricing: $30/month for 5,000 subscribers, $70/month for 20,000. Start at the free tier, upgrade when you hit 5k.

For email deliverability, set up an **ActiveCampaign** account. Connect it to Beehiiv via Zapier (one‑time $20/month). Use ActiveCampaign’s segmentation to tag subscribers by “AI Focus” or “E‑commerce Interest.” Add a double opt‑in email to keep spam scores low.

Add a voice hook: In **ElevenLabs**, generate a 30‑second intro using the voice “Joanna.” Export the MP3 and embed it into the newsletter header via Beehiiv’s “Add Audio” feature. Users will hear a personalized intro every time they open the email.

HACK: Use **Grammarly**’s API to auto‑review each email before sending. Set “Tone: Friendly, Professional” and “Audience: Business.” The output will flag passive voice and wordiness, keeping your copy crisp.

### Step 4: Automate Publishing & Monetize (1‑2 hrs)

Create a publishing schedule in **Buffer**. Connect Beehiiv to Buffer via Zapier, set up a Zap that fires every Monday at 9 am, posting the newsletter to LinkedIn, Twitter, and your personal blog. Buffer’s plan: $25/month for 3 social accounts. Use LinkedIn’s “native share” feature to attach the PDF as a Post, increasing reach.

Monetize with **Klaviyo**. Build a drip campaign that delivers a free 3‑day “AI Newsletter Starter Kit.” Klaviyo’s pricing: $10/month for 2,000 contacts. Use the “Smart Send” feature to target users who clicked the intro audio but haven’t subscribed. Offer a $99/month tier for premium content and a 20% discount for the first month.

Finally, schedule a weekly strategy call via **Calendly**. Let prospects book 15‑minute demos directly from your newsletter link. Calendly’s integration with Zapier will auto‑create calendar events and add them to your Google Calendar, preventing double booking.

HACK: Automate the revenue split. Use **Zapier** to trigger a Google Sheets row whenever a subscription goes live. Connect that sheet to a quick payment processor (Stripe integration) and set a 70/30 split. You’ll see revenue instantly in a dashboard you build

## Getting Clients

You can’t get a $22k/mo AI newsletter business by waiting for people to find you. You have to go out and grab them. Below are three proven ways, each with a realistic conversion rate, that have worked for dozens of solo founders in 2026.

### Method 1: LinkedIn Lead‑Bashing (Conversion Rate: 12%)

LinkedIn still dominates B2B. Start by building a list of 500 tech‑savvy decision‑makers with PhantomBuster’s “LinkedIn Profile Scraper” ($49/mo). Feed that list into Apollo.io for email enrichment and company data ($99/mo). Use Zapier to push the enriched records into a Google Sheet that feeds a Slack channel. Inside Slack, run a ChatGPT script that drafts a personalized InMail greeting. Send the InMail through LinkedIn Premium ($29.99/mo) and schedule three follow‑ups with a 48‑hour cadence. Track opens and replies with HubSpot’s free email tracking. A 12% conversion means you’ll land ~60 paid clients after about 500 outreach attempts—roughly 0.12 conversion is what the industry average says is achievable with a well‑personalized approach. The key is to keep the messaging short and value‑heavy: “We automate your newsletter so you can focus on closing deals.”

### Method 2: Content Funnel on Medium + Twitter (Conversion Rate: 8%)

Publish a 1,500‑word Medium post titled “Why Every CEO Needs an AI Newsletter.” Embed a Beehiiv opt‑in form that captures email and phone. Use Canva ($12.99/mo) to design a snazzy header image; use Replit to spin up a simple static landing page that redirects to the opt‑in. Schedule 3‑5 tweets a day via Buffer’s free tier to amplify the post. Every tweet links back to the landing page and includes a short LinkedIn article snippet that hints at the deeper content available for subscribers. Within 48 hours, run a ChatGPT‑crafted welcome email series that nurtures the leads with case studies. An 8% conversion from the Medium post means that 125 out of 1,500 readers will sign up for your paid service, yielding 125 paying clients after a few weeks of nurturing.

### Method 3: Workshops & Partnerships (Conversion Rate: 6%)

Host a 90‑minute virtual workshop titled “Automate Your Newsletter in 3 Hours.” Use Calendly (free) to let prospects book slots. Record a Loom demo ($20/mo) that you’ll share as a follow‑up. Sell tickets on Shopify Basic ($29/mo) with a +10% discount for repeat bookings. After the workshop, auto‑add attendees to ActiveCampaign ($29/mo) and trigger a drip series that upsells a full AI‑newsletter service. A 6% conversion means that for every 200 workshop attendees, 12 will become paying clients. The advantage? You get a ready‑made audience that already trusts you, and you can upsell a high‑margin package.

## What Nobody Warns You About

**1. The “Free” Tier is a Speed Bump, Not a Freedom**

You’ll start with Beehiiv’s free plan. It feels great until the 5,000‑subscriber cap kicks in. When that line hits, you pay $19/month for the Pro tier. That’s just the start. Your email list will need regular deliverability checks, so you’ll eventually pay for a tool like Mailgun ($10/month) or SendGrid ($14.95/month). The reality is: a free stack saves money now but costs more in the long run once you need to push beyond a few thousand readers.  

**2. AI Content Isn’t a 10‑Minute Click‑and‑Sell**

Running GPT‑4 on Replit costs $20/month, and that’s only the GPU. You still have to spend 2–3 hours weekly curating prompts, editing, and fact‑checking. Make.com automation can save 30 % of that time, but you’ll pay $29/month for a paid plan. If you skip the human touch, your newsletters will read like spam, and subscribers will churn.  

**3. Spam Filters Are the Real Gatekeepers**

Every AI voice you generate with ElevenLabs or Vapi runs the risk of hitting spam filters. Even a perfectly edited text can get blocked if the subject line looks like a bot. You’ll need a deliverability service that costs $20/month, and you’ll still have to tweak your sending pattern every few weeks. Trusting the AI to write flawless copy is a false sense of security.  

**4. Underpricing Is the Silent Killer of Revenue**

You’ll see courses telling you to charge $50/month per subscriber. That math breaks when you factor in platform fees: Beehiiv takes 10 %, Hostinger adds 3 % for SSL, and Klaviyo charges $15/month for the first 500 contacts. You’ll need to charge at least $200 per client to cover costs and still make profit. Use Apollo.io to target the right prospects, but don’t let the “cheap” price lure you into a losing business model.
